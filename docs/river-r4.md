# River R4 presentation

R4 makes the world-owned spine and the R3 carving cross-section authoritative for rendered river water. Water is a four-unit-wide ribbon at Y = -0.18; it is not a flood fill. Terrain remains the visible bank, so coarse, polygonal shoreline intersections are an accepted temporary limitation pending post-R4 visual inspection.

Tight-bend validation found that the former `(65, 36)` control point made the mathematical two-unit inner offset self-intersect. The authored point is minimally softened to `(62, 34)`; the bend remains pronounced, while both constant-width offset edges are simple and all water triangles retain positive area.

The water uses a one-world-unit global arc-length lattice. An immutable lattice, strip-interval list, interval bounds, and 16-unit spatial index are built once per spine and retained in a `WeakMap`. Each chunk queries only its index cells and deterministically clips the bounded candidate triangles to the exact chunk rectangle. Boundary vertices and distance-based UVs are therefore identical regardless of streaming or generation order. A wet chunk owns one batched mesh, and its geometry follows normal chunk disposal; a dry chunk creates no water object. The material is shared by the mesh factory.

The legacy `river` record remains generated for bridges, collision/classification, vegetation exclusion, POIs, and other R5 consumers. Its old water ribbon and bank/channel meshes are no longer instantiated. Standalone lake and wetland presentation remains unchanged. Detailed river debug mode shows the mathematical channel/falloff edges, chunk grid, frames, and every global water lattice sample.

At one-unit spacing the 335-unit authored fixture has 335 global intervals (670 triangles). Before the bounded index, every wet chunk processed all 335 intervals. After indexing, tested intersecting chunks process at most 25 candidates (19 in the representative crossed chunk), while dry chunks process zero. A representative 3×3 neighborhood around `(3, 2)` has five water meshes/draw calls and 152 clipped triangles; there is no per-frame rebuild. Local microbenchmarks (1,000 cached generations) measured about 0.0017 ms for a dry chunk and 0.076 ms for that crossed chunk, compared with the old algorithm's 335-interval scan per crossed chunk. Exact clipping avoids transparent overlap and its memory is limited to intersecting fragments. Runtime frame-rate measurement and shoreline refinement remain visual follow-up work.
