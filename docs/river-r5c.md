# River and Night R5c: POIs and navigation

## Milestone and legacy audit

R5a world-river bridges and R5b vegetation/general-object exclusion are complete. R5c migrates POI hydrology and navigation; R5d is next and owns player water classification, swimming/movement, safe-player-position semantics, and final legacy-river removal. R6 performs the full seam, streaming, regeneration, and generation-order validation after R5d. Procedural macro paths, secondary meanders, variable width, river-connected/standalone lakes, day/night, and discovered-POI lanterns remain deferred. The governing rule remains: **all active river consumers must use the world-owned river before any of those river features are introduced.**

The pre-R5c audit found legacy `isRiverAt` use in POI centre/footprint rejection, wetland-cabin dry clearing checks, lake shoreline selection, and `distanceToRiver`; the biome debug river guide targeted the fixed column edge. No archetype used a river-facing porch, river dock, across-river mission/collectible record, or bridge-adjacent landmark. The lake house and its dock are standalone-lake-owned and remain so. Farmhouse, forest cabin, and highland watchtower avoid the river. There is no registered mountain cottage. Bridges already used the world river and canonical POIs. Ordinary collectible placement remains R5b-owned. No POI depended on caller generation order: globally addressed cells and ranking were already canonical.

## Shared relationship and signed side

`worldRiverRelationship.ts` exposes a presentation-neutral point query plus a reusable bounded context. The context asks the spine spatial index once for nearby segments and has an explicit empty/dry fast path. A record contains nearest point, normalized progress and arc distance, centreline and signed distances, tangent/left normal, water/shore/walkable-bank/outer extents, distances to water and bank, same-side lip landing point, indexed segment, endpoint state, and local curvature/threshold state. Positive signed side is river-left: tangent follows increasing river distance and normal is `(-tangent.z, tangent.x)`. This is stable on vertical, horizontal, diagonal, bent, and cross-chunk reaches.

POI generation creates one 16-world-unit-expanded context covering its canonical candidate neighborhood and reuses it. Relationship objects are immutable plain data, computed during candidate evaluation, and copied to accepted POIs; rendering, navigation, and debug do not resample the river. There is no Three.js dependency, scene traversal, full-spine scan per candidate, or per-frame POI river query.

## Category, footprint, and orientation rules

* **River-avoiding:** plains farmhouse reserves **2.5 wu**, forest cabin **1.5 wu**, and highland watchtower **1.0 wu** beyond the authoritative walkable-bank edge. All oriented footprint samples—not merely the centre—must clear that boundary. The outer terrain falloff is explicitly not blanket exclusion.
* **Standalone-lake-owned:** lake house retains its radial lake shoreline search, lake-facing rotation, dock, lake surface elevation, and lake collision/rendering contract. Its candidate and dry-land checks now reject the world-river water footprint, never the legacy column. It is not silently converted to a river house.
* **River-adjacent/facing:** none currently exists. The serializable relationship type, preferred-facing vector, water-facing anchor, landing point, and category union are future-safe hooks, but R5c does not invent an archetype or deform terrain to force one.

Existing dry-biome orientation is preserved for avoiding/unaffected buildings; it is not falsely made river-facing. The same stored POI rotation still drives rendering, collision, shadow, entrance, and oriented zones. A future river-facing archetype must use the relationship normal and its documented asset-axis correction. A future river dock must start at the authoritative lip/landing, follow the same-side normal, reach the water without crossing the opposite bank, use the authoritative river water elevation, and share one footprint with rendering and collision.

Every generated POI now stores one finite `navigationAnchor`; current buildings use the entrance rather than a chunk coordinate. The guide reads that anchor (with compatibility fallback for retained old records), ignores non-finite targets, derives distance and screen direction from player/camera-to-anchor geometry, clamps to the overlay edge, and clears/hides stale or absent targets. The biome debug river cue now points to the nearest world-spine location and hides inside the controlled river environment, including reaches outside legacy column zero.

## Bridge composition, ownership, and debug

POIs have deterministic priority: canonical POIs are generated independently first; canonical bridges then query those POIs and reject building conflicts. The bridge compatibility obstacle argument is intentionally ignored. Thus `generatePois` never calls bridge generation, `generateBridges` cannot recursively re-enter itself through POIs, and caller order cannot alter accepted data. Chunk ownership remains the candidate centre/POI origin exactly once; neighboring generation may consume zones without cloning ownership. Bridge solids, construction clearance, and approaches continue to participate in the combined R5a/R5b exclusion pass after both canonical sets are resolved.

POI detailed debug remains lazy and chunk-bounded. One batched line geometry now includes the POI-to-nearest-river line, local tangent/normal, and navigation anchor alongside existing oriented zones, entrances, and docks. Accepted/rejected candidates remain instanced batches with reason and owner metadata in their plain candidate records. Debug-off chunks retain none of those presentation records, and the renderer does not duplicate the river-spine debug view.

## Performance and remaining R5d work

Candidate identity, rarity, and coarse biome rejection still precede terrain and river work. A normal search evaluates at most the existing canonical cell neighborhood (typically 147 ordinary-archetype plus 81 watchtower slots before cheap rejection); only river-avoiding candidates surviving cheap gates issue relationship/footprint queries, all against the shared bounded segment set. Dry contexts return before candidate geometry refinement. A ten-seed uncached development sample measured **41.60 ms/chunk** in a distant dry region and **94.44 ms/chunk** in a river-crossed region; the sampled owner chunks contained no accepted candidate because POI cells are intentionally sparse. This is a local diagnostic, not a benchmark contract. Navigation adds no river work per POI frame; it reads stored anchors. The only per-frame world-river nearest query is the explicitly enabled biome debug river cue.

The remaining legacy river consumers for R5d are `terrainSampling.sampleRiverCrossSection`/`isRiverAt` gameplay surface classification and callers in player movement, swimming-depth behavior, and safe-position logic, plus legacy compatibility river generation/render records. They are deliberately not removed here. Standalone lakes remain separately owned.

Permanent R5c regression coverage is consolidated into parameterized relationship-frame/index tests, existing POI determinism/ownership tests, navigation stale-target/camera/clamping tests, and existing bridge canonical-conflict/generation-order suites. No temporary helper-detail tests remain and no extended suite was necessary.
